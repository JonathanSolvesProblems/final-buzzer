# Devpost gallery

Rendered by `devpost/slides/build.py` from live captures and the results files. Upload in this order; paste each caption under its image (the caption is not drawn on the slide).

1. **01-board.png**: Saturday's 34 games replayed live through the agent and the PayPal sandbox: 28 paid, 81 officials, $3,145, a median of 40.2 seconds from the saved score to PayPal's confirmation. Every tile on the board is a game and its face is its money state. Live at finalbuzzer.jonathansolvesproblems.com.
2. **02-drawer.png**: Tap a paid tile and the drawer shows the path from the buzzer to the money, from the ledger's own record of the run: 0.0 s the score is saved, 1.6 s the agent asks for the sheet, the rate card, PayPal's record and the officials' day, 5.9 s it chooses to pay and the gate checks every amount, 39.4 s PayPal confirms all three officials paid. Who was paid, how much, and the PayPal batch to copy.
3. **03-phones.png**: The scorer's page on a phone, and Official D's own page: every game worked, what it paid, how soon after the buzzer PayPal confirmed it, the folds into the evening payout, and what is next on the sheet. Score a game that has not started on the live site and watch its officials get paid.
4. **04-held.png**: Three of Saturday's 34 games were held for Sam: a score saved twice, which PayPal already showed paid (the agent sent nothing more); a forfeit, whose show-up fee the rate card does not carry yet; and a game with no table official on the sheet. Each shows one sentence and the choices that make sense; Sam's tap pays through the same gate and the same PayPal call as the agent.
5. **05-words.png**: Each decision is a schema-checked tool call (src/agent/decision.ts) with a reason in the scorer's words. Three of Saturday's 34, as recorded: a plain payment after reading PayPal's record, a fold for officials who asked to be paid once a day, and a question for Sam about a missing table official.
6. **06-how.png**: How it is built: a score saved on a phone, Claude reading four things and choosing one of three actions, a code gate on every amount, then PayPal Payouts, Invoicing through the MCP server, Transaction Search and Webhooks, landing in the officials' accounts, their own pages, the board and the money page.
7. **07-live.png**: Game 3 was scored on the public site on 9 November 2026 and its officials were paid in 41.2 seconds, with the agent on duty: it asked for the sheet, the rate card and PayPal's record at 3.0 s, chose at 6.6 s, and PayPal confirmed at 41.2 s. Five webhook events arrived signature-verified (the batch processing, the batch success, one per official); every event the receiver has seen is listed at /api/webhooks.
8. **08-invoices.png**: The money page's entry fees, sorted by reminders: 52 teams invoiced through PayPal's MCP server, 50 paid, 32 reminders, one invoice split into a deposit and a balance at a manager's request, one spot released to the waitlist a week after the due date. 57 decisions over 33 days, each the agent's choice with PayPal's record in hand.
9. **09-replan.png**: Sunday, Gym E closed from 9:00, 10 games affected (Gym EF shares its floor). The agent moved 9, each move checked in code against the schedule's rules (team rest, playoff order, joined courts sharing a floor), and refunded the two teams of the game that could not be played, $175 each, through PayPal Payouts. results/replan.json.
10. **10-reconcile.png**: Reconciliation against PayPal: all 99 payout items the ledger holds appear in PayPal's transaction search, matched by item id, across 30 games and the evening batches: $3,795 CAD confirmed, $31.68 in payout fees, nothing missing.
11. **11-ablations.png**: Two comparisons committed in the repository, on the same 34 games: with PayPal switched off the agent held every game and paid nobody; a fixed rule in place of the model pays everyone on the sheet, loses the once-a-day folds and asks Sam nothing, while the code gate stops the forfeit and the missing official whoever decides. A script reads every figure in the write-up back from the results and fails when one disagrees.
12. **12-why.png**: Why the buzzer: in the 2023 National Officiating Survey 18.3% of officials said they are paid what they are worth; a Minnesota referee told Prep Network in March 2026 that colleagues are paid months late or never; in September 2026 a Pennsylvania recreation commission told the Altoona Mirror its referees wait two weeks for a cheque. On 8 November 2026 I wrote to that commission to offer a sandbox run.
