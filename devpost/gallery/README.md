# Devpost gallery

Rendered by `tmp/slides/build.py` from live captures and the results files. Upload in this order; paste each caption under its image.

1. **01-board.png**: Saturday's 34 games replayed live through the agent and the PayPal sandbox: 28 paid, 81 officials, $3,145, a median of 40.2 seconds from the saved score to PayPal's confirmation. Every tile is a game and its face is its money state. Live at finalbuzzer.jonathansolvesproblems.com.
2. **02-drawer.png**: Tap a paid tile and the drawer shows the path from the buzzer to the money: 0.0 s the score is saved, 5.9 s the agent has read the sheet, the rate card and PayPal's record and sent the pay, 39.4 s PayPal confirms all three officials paid. Who was paid, how much, and the PayPal batch to copy.
3. **03-phones.png**: The scorer's page on a phone, and the board's drawer on a phone. Score a game that has not started on the live site and watch its officials get paid; a held game waits for Sam's PIN.
4. **04-held-17.png**: Game 17's score was saved a second time. The agent read PayPal's record, saw the game paid 31 seconds earlier, and held it with nothing more sent. On the board Sam's only choice is “Right, nothing more”.
5. **05-held-30.png**: Three games were held for Sam on Saturday: a score saved twice, a forfeit with no fee on the card, and a game with no table official on the sheet. Each shows one sentence and the choices that make sense; the tap pays through the same gate and the same PayPal call as the agent.
6. **06-words.png**: Each decision is a schema-checked tool call (src/agent/decision.ts) with a reason in the scorer's words. Five of Saturday's 34, as recorded: a hold on PayPal's record, a plain payment, a fold for officials who asked to be paid once a day, a forfeit, a missing official.
7. **07-how.png**: How it is built: a score saved on a phone, Claude reading four things and choosing one of three actions, a code gate on every amount, then PayPal Payouts, Invoicing through the MCP server, Transaction Search and Webhooks.
8. **08-invoices.png**: The money page's entry fees: 52 teams invoiced through PayPal's MCP server, 50 paid, 32 reminders, one invoice split into a deposit and a balance at a manager's request, one spot released to the waitlist.
9. **09-replan.png**: Sunday, Gym E closed from 9:00, 10 games affected. The agent moved 9, each move checked in code against the schedule's rules (team rest, playoff order, joined courts sharing a floor), and refunded the two teams of the game that could not be played, $175 each, through PayPal Payouts. results/replan.json.
10. **10-reconcile.png**: Reconciliation against PayPal: all 99 payout items the ledger holds appear in PayPal's transaction search, matched by item id, across 30 games and the evening batches: $3,795 CAD confirmed, $31.68 in payout fees, nothing missing.
11. **11-ablations.png**: Two comparisons committed in the repository: PayPal switched off (the agent held all 34 games and paid nobody) and a fixed rule in place of the model (the once-a-day folds lost). A script reads every figure in the write-up back from the results and fails when one disagrees.
12. **12-why.png**: Why the buzzer: in the 2023 National Officiating Survey 18.3% of officials said they are paid what they are worth; in September 2026 a Pennsylvania recreation commission told the Altoona Mirror its referees wait two weeks for a cheque. On 8 November 2026 I wrote to that commission to offer a sandbox run.
